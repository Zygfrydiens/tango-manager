## How this teacher works (applies to every video)

- **Format:** same warm-up every time (shoulder/arm circles, torso side-stretch, standing torso rotation, forward fold + bounce, hip circles, calf raises, calf stretch), then 4–8 progressive exercises, each done **slowly first then half-time (2×)**, usually **with wall-fingertip support first, then on your own axis**.
- **Metronome:** 58 bpm, 2 beats/bar. "Down" = beat 1, "up" = beat 2. He counts only downbeats. Drilling to a metronome (not music) is the point — nothing hides bad timing.
- **Recurring cues:** push the standing leg into the floor "like a screwdriver" to control pivots; keep arms low/in the embrace frame; do everything **low** (knees bent) for balance; **keep the free leg always moving** (no freezing between positions); separate movements cleanly (leg, then torso, then hip) when slow.
- **Two foot positions he names:** *cuchillo/"knife"* (forward step — outside edge of foot down, little toe), *"pretty"/zero* (inside edge down), *"toes"* (back step — foot extended, toes down, heel kept **inside**, never splayed out).
- **Big mental models:** the free leg is a **double pendulum** (hip pendulum = leg in any direction; knee pendulum = lower leg front/back); the two ways to walk are **projection** (free foot leads through the floor) and **pendulum** (leg swings); use **as little force as possible** — let the leg's weight do the work.

## Drills by topic (mined videos)

### Posture & axis (v01, v26, v31)
- **Rib-cage placement (v01/v31):** cross fingers, turn palms down, straighten elbows, raise arms overhead and "push the ceiling" → ribcage lifts up/forward and weight shifts onto the front of the feet (tango posture). Then lower the arms but **keep the torso where it is**. 4× slow.
- **Wall roll-down (v01):** hips against wall, roll the spine onto the wall vertebra by vertebra from the low back up to the head, then peel off head-first. Then the reverse: roll the hip forward off the wall first. Builds a straight back.
- **Stick shoulder-opener (v01):** wide grip on a stick, pass it from front → overhead → behind and back, narrowing grip over time. Opens shoulders/embrace.

### Balance (v01, v32)
- **4-point standing-leg series (v01), one leg, 8× each:** (1) knee up → extend leg back, toe floor → bend standing leg to floor → return; (2) same but free foot stays off the floor the whole time, bend/stretch standing leg without leaning the torso; (3) cross free leg behind, lower on both legs, open, cross other side; (4) bend standing leg while extending free leg to the side (flexed foot), keep hips square.
- **Calf-raise hold:** feet together, bounce ×3, rise onto toes, hold 3s, lower **slowly** (control the descent). Strengthens ankles for one-leg balance.

### The walk — projection (v27) & pendulum (v33)
- **Projection (v27):** free foot leads through the floor in the step direction. Drill each direction (side / front / back) × slow then fast, bending the standing leg as you project and stretching it as you close. Add **dissociation** (turn torso toward standing leg one rep, toward free leg the next). Optionally shift axis slightly onto the free foot ("intention of going"). Foot positions: front = knife, back = toes (heel inside).
- **Pendulum (v33):** the free leg as a **double pendulum**. (1) hip pendulum front-back, knee straight, minimum force, foot stays on floor; (2) side pendulum open/close; (3) floor pendulum front→back→front; (4) knee pendulum only (knees together, swing lower leg). 32 reps each, "use the weight of the leg, not muscle." This is why a relaxed walk looks dynamic (fast through vertical, slow at the ends, like a child's swing).

### Steps / size (v05)
- **Big side step = 4 moves:** open free leg + bend standing leg → push floor & slide → transfer weight parallel to floor (use knees, don't bob/tilt) → close & stretch. **Small step = 3 moves** (no bend/push; legs stay straight). Then adapt the same to front & back steps. Combine into a sequence (small/big/big forward, side, three back, etc.), slow then double-time.

### Weight transfer (v04)
- Four kinds of weight change, each drilled to metronome then with a 90° pivot after each: (1) basic in place; (2) cross (one foot crossed in front, step on front foot); (3) change-of-leg front (free foot circles tight & small around standing foot to the front each time); (4) change-of-leg back. Cues: feet together, **tiny tight circle** around the standing foot (no big arcs — no time when fast), foot flat, stay on one spot.

### Free-leg control & strength (v03, v16, v33)
- **Foot-position roll (v03):** free leg extended front/side/back, roll the foot/ankle 16× each direction; then learn knife/pretty/toes positions and move between zero→front→side→back keeping the leg always moving; then add pivots after each.
- **Swing + knee combinations (v16), wall then free, 8×4 each:** (1) straight-knee hip swing; (2) bend knee on the back swing (foot lifts behind — let the foot's weight bend it); (3) bend knee on the front swing (knee hits your hand held at hip height); (4) both; (5) add a cross when swinging back (inertia bends the knee against the standing leg); (6) full combination back-knee/close-knee/cross. "Use as little force as possible; let the rebound help." Take lower-back breaks (forward fold) between sets.

### Dissociation (v25, embedded in v14/v27)
- Standing dissociation, walk-with-dissociation, ocho marking from dissociation only (no arms). Rule: **no matter where the torso turns, the free leg keeps going in the same line** — that's what "dissociation" means. Make the torsion happen **low, just above the hip**, not in the shoulders; head moves with the torso (you're following a partner).

### Pivots (v01, v03, v04, v08, v18)
- Prep: weight on one foot, free foot a few cm off floor, legs tight together, turn torso L–center–R (8× each leg) — engages core/dissociation. Then 90° pivots: torso leads, then hip follows, **press standing foot into floor like a screwdriver** for control. Drill pivots both *toward* the standing leg (natural) and *away* from it (harder, but needed for lápiz/sacada entries).

### Ochos (v14 back, v15 front)
- **Two techniques, both leader-relevant:** **hip-first** (rotate hip → project leg → transfer → close; big, dynamic, open-embrace; this is your **back sacada** mechanic) and **leg-first** (relaxed leg circles back and *the leg's swing turns the hip*; small, intimate, close-embrace; this is the **"catch her foot" for a gancho/pasada** mechanic). Drill each: half-ocho slow → half-ocho half-time → full ocho slow → full ocho half-time, wall then free. Cues: keep arms **forward** when the hip opens (or you fall back); stay **low**; on the step turn the foot **outside** (big toe down, heel in) so it supports the next pivot.

### Giros (v17 sequences, v19 elements, v20 combination)
- The man walks **forward with pivots** inside the woman's front-side-back giro. Base sequence: **T-step (90°) + 180° pivot + front step**, repeated — 8 steps = one full revolution. Cues: do it **low**, **torso anticipates** the next direction (always slightly ahead/spiral), on the 180° pivot **leave the foot behind** then close. Variations: add a change-of-weight on the T (front with the same leg); add a **cross-back front-sacada** on her side step; combine both tricks. All tricks happen on **her side step**. Transition (front→back→side step) flips the giro direction.

### Adornments — leader (v02 helicóptero, v13 lápiz, v09 ✗)
- **Lápiz (v13):** free-leg big toe "draws" circles on the floor (here front→back). Drills: smooth instantaneous weight-change ("light switch" — never weight on both feet); 8 lápices per leg with cross-back to switch; lápiz + pivot (toward, then away from the lápiz direction); **double lápiz** = tiny fast knee-circle + big slow hip-lápiz (a beautiful leader adornment, big dynamic contrast). Keep arms low, stand low, separate leg/torso/hip.
- **Helicóptero (v02):** the leader's version is a low adornment built from the woman's structure — back step + boleo-forward (low, lower-leg brushing the other shin) + change weight + boleo-forward + step, optionally with a tiny jump. Practiced as `step / boleo(+small jump) / change / boleo / step` to metronome.

### Musicality — rhythm (v22)
A complete rhythm curriculum, danced over one trivial step (forward-change-back-change) so all focus is on timing:
- **Beat + active/passive leg:** strong beat = active (leading) leg, weak beat = passive leg (still accented, just less). Step *exactly on* the beat, even slightly late — we all anticipate; "dance slower than you think."
- **Double time:** one subdivision between beats.
- **Half time / pause:** skip beats; don't freeze — do a **slow weight transfer** through the pause and bend down to build tension toward the resolution.
- **Syncopation:** accent (a step) where there's normally none — the "and", or (with the beat in 4) the 2nd/3rd/4th subdivision. Includes a sophisticated drill mixing syncopations + double-times across a divided beat.
- **Triplets:** beat split in 3 (≠ vals, which is 3 *beats* grouped). Then combine triplet + double-time in one sequence.
- Practice tip: **clap the rhythm first** (no feet), then transfer to the feet; check on video / with a partner.

### Embrace (v30)
Fixes the common milonga mistakes — gold for a leader:
- **Approach chest-first, arms down** — walking up with arms half-raised reads as insecurity / a barrier. Place the chest at distance, *then* the arms.
- **Contact point:** touch the partner with the **ribs below the chest (sternum end)**, not the upper chest (which crowds the head and pushes the hip back).
- **Arms travel in a curve, not a straight line:** open the arms and use **elbows/shoulders** to wrap into the embrace (leader's curve is more horizontal; follower's more vertical). To release, **rewind** the same curve — don't drop the arms.
- Drill: ribcage-placement exercise → wall approach (touch with ribs) → elbow-circle arm path → full sequence (place chest, curve into embrace, rewind out).
